# Temperature Analysis Insights

## 1. Dataset Size

The Baltimore temperature dataset contains 1968 records and 20 columns.

## 2. Temperature Sensor Columns

The dataset contains temperature readings from multiple sensors, including:

- H16
- H17
- H18
- H19
- L17
- L18
- L20
- M16
- M18
- M19
- VH16
- VH17
- VH18
- VH19
- VL16
- VL17
- VL18
- VL19
- VL20

The `Date - Time` and `hour` columns provide time-related information.

## 3. Mean Temperature

The mean temperature represents the average temperature recorded by each sensor.

Comparing the mean values helps identify sensors that generally record higher or lower temperatures.

## 4. Minimum Temperature

The minimum temperature shows the lowest temperature recorded by each sensor during the observation period.

## 5. Maximum Temperature

The maximum temperature shows the highest temperature recorded by each sensor.

## 6. Standard Deviation

Standard deviation shows how much the temperature readings vary around their average.

A higher standard deviation indicates greater temperature variation, while a lower value indicates more stable readings.

## 7. Highest and Lowest Mean Temperature

The sensor with the highest mean temperature represents an area that was generally warmer.

The sensor with the lowest mean temperature represents an area that was generally cooler.

## 8. Microclimate Variation

The differences between sensor means, minimum values, maximum values, and standard deviations show that temperature can vary between different locations.

These variations are useful for understanding local microclimate patterns and can support further microclimate analysis.
